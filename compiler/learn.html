PROGRAMMING IN CAS / BEGINNER GUIDE

Describe an experience.
Understand its structure.

CAS gives you a way to describe how a person relates to an idea: what is happening, where it happens in their experience, what motivates it, and how it is expressed. An expression makes those choices visible so you can inspect, discuss and revise them.

Start with the situation

Programming in CAS introduces four questions: the operation, the domain, the drive and the register. Use them to make a written interpretation precise.

Inner experience

Thoughts, emotions, beliefs, attention and intentions. What idea is present? Does the person feel drawn to it, resist it, examine it or encounter it repeatedly? What matters to them?

Outer experience

Other people, environments, events and social conditions. What was said? What happened? Which demands, opportunities or constraints surround the person? Record these details alongside the expression.

The relationship is the useful part. The same comment can be read as guidance, criticism or rejection. A belief can shape that interpretation; an interpretation can accompany a feeling; an intention can guide an action. CAS helps you state which relationship you are modeling and compare alternatives.

  1. Describe the observed event in ordinary words.
  2. Write your interpretation separately: the thought, feeling, motive or intention to examine.
  3. Choose a Noetic, domain, drive and register. Explain each choice.
  4. Run the expression. Inspect the result, then change a choice and compare.
Where might I use this?
Psychology and self-reflection
Describe a proposed relationship among a belief, an appraisal, a feeling and an action.
Sociology
Discuss how roles, shared expectations and social pressure relate to identity, belonging and choices.
Spirituality
Articulate accounts of purpose, values, intention and practice in the vocabulary of the person or tradition studied.
Education
Compare interest, understanding and practice; teach symbolic language with editable examples.
Systems thinking and teamwork
Separate goals, assumptions, dependencies and actions in a shared situation.

These are intended study and modeling uses. You supply and review the human interpretation; the compiler checks and processes the notation.

Relationships: Maya hears criticism from a friend

No phrase selected.

The human situation

Maya has spent her Sunday making a poster for a neighborhood fundraiser. She sends it to Jon, a close friend who volunteered to help. He replies, “The headline is hard to read.” This is an invented teaching scene; Maya’s interpretation is stated explicitly so you can examine it.

  1. What happens

    Maya reads Jon’s message twice. He has commented on the headline; he has not said he rejects her.

  2. What she makes of it

    Maya’s immediate appraisal is . She recoils from the criticism: .

  3. Why it matters and how it feels

    She thinks, . Her experience is .

  4. What she does

    She closes the chat and leaves Jon’s message unanswered. The silence belongs to the story surrounding the state we are modeling.

One selected moment, in CAS

Read the complete expression as Maya’s aversion to the criticism, within her appraisal of it, with connection to Jon at stake and hurt in the foreground. It joins four descriptions of this selected moment. Jon’s words, Maya’s interpretation and her later silence remain distinct parts of the written account.

Match every token to the story

^3Operator: Negative

In this story

“I do not want to hear this criticism”

Maya is repelled by the idea of the criticism. ^3 identifies that aversion; it does not establish that Jon actually rejected her.

^EDomain: Evaluative

In this story

“This means he is rejecting my work”

This is Maya’s appraisal of what the message means. Evaluative locates the state in judgment and felt significance.

_{F4}Drive: Connection

In this story

“Jon is a close friend, and I want us to stay close”

The relationship with this particular friend is what matters in this reading. F4 identifies Connection as the motive.

|fRegister: Affective

In this story

“I feel hurt and want to withdraw”

Lowercase f marks the feeling-toned manner of the state. It describes the hurt; it does not order Maya to withdraw.

Punctuation: how the four parts join

  • ^ introduces an operator number, as in ^3, or a domain letter, as in ^E.
  • _ attaches the drive; { and } enclose its F code. Thus _{F4} attaches Connection.
  • | introduces the register. Its letter is case-sensitive: |f is Affective; |E is Embodied.

Read left to right: operator, domain, drive, register. The punctuation connects those choices; it adds no hidden event or instruction.

What you will see when you run it

The Result reports dominant_state: E3, dominant_operator: CO-3, dominant_drive: F4, register_name: Affective. Here, “dominant” is the compiler field name for the single state supplied.

Read the symbols as a sentence

^Noetic^Domain_{Drive}|Register asks: what operation, where, why, and in what way? A state pairs a Noetic with a domain. Drive and register add detail. Ten Noetics and four domains give 40 core state positions.

The book calls the Noetic the “verb.” Use the compiler column for this Site’s output. The last column records the exact names in the book’s Chapter 2 and Appendix A. Both sources assign names to the same written ^0…^9 positions; this compiler implements one operator set.

Female and Male are the glossary’s names for ideas composing a mind and ideas composing an idea. Here they name structural operations.

Four domains — where
CodeName and meaning
^PPrincipled: Values, identity and guiding commitments.
^CCognitive: Thought, reasoning and mental representations.
^EEvaluative: Appraisal, judgment and felt significance.
^OOperational: Action and execution.

The framework orders these P → C → E → O: a principle can inform a thought, an appraisal and an action.

Seven drives — why

“Drive” and “Foundation” name the same motivational categories. _{F4} attaches Connection to a state.

CodeDrive and meaning
F1Unity: Wholeness and coherence.
F2Wisdom: Understanding, insight and learning.
F3Vitality: Life, energy and thriving.
F4Connection: Relationship, attachment and relating to others.
F5Agency: Chosen action, capability and self-direction.
F6Structure: Order, organization and dependable form.
F7Purpose: Meaning and direction.

These names agree with the book. F2 is Wisdom and F3 is Vitality; some older example captions misname F2.

Four registers — how
CodeName and meaning
|TTranscendent: Broad, integrative and purpose-oriented.
|AAnalytical: Explicit, sequential and reason-based.
|fAffective: Feeling-toned and value-laden.
|EEmbodied: Concrete, sensorimotor engagement.

^E is the Evaluative domain; |E is the Embodied register. Affective uses lowercase f. |⟨A,f,T⟩ lists several registers: commas separate them; angle brackets enclose them.

Combine expressions

Choose the operation expressing the relationship you intend. Parentheses group expressions; commas separate arguments or list members.

+ — Co-presence
^3^P|A + ^3^P|f
