Read the colors.

The editor calls the original terminal's _highlight_parts function. Its colors identify notation categories. They do not measure strength, validity, truth or confidence. Every category also has a text label in the editor's legend and cursor readout.

^2^C_{F2}|T means the Positive operator, Cognitive domain, Wisdom drive and Transcendent register. These names come from the compiler's dictionary; they describe the CAS framework.

Short form C2 remains plain because the original highlighter does not assign it the explicit ^2^C colors. Coloring is separate from parsing. CCDL uses plain text here. To keep typing positions accurate, the browser retains your two-character -> instead of replacing it with the terminal's single arrow glyph. Live colors pause above 16 KiB; compilation accepts 64 KiB.

Cube colors mean something different

The inherited cube palette groups diagram roles: blue is the added layout root; teal is a CAS container; cyan is an identifier; violet is an operator/call; green is a target or output; amber is a fractal/step; rose is an assertion/confidence category. The face labels and inspector give the role in words. A rose cube is not measured confidence.

The 2D map has its own node-kind palette: noetics cyan, domains emerald, drives amber, registers pink, states sky blue, calls/operators purple and targets/emergents lavender. Thus an Ω target can be orange in syntax and lavender in the map. The Site does not assign one meaning to every occurrence of a color.

Source trail

Syntax: cas_terminal.py, THEME, _highlight_parts, span; the same category rules exist in the parent compiler's CompilerPage.highlightSyntax. The active and frozen terminal highlighter bodies were compared and are identical.

Cube roles: cas_cubes.py, PALETTE and _kind, adapted from the parent sggs_palette.ts. Map roles: cas_svg.py, _color. Exact shipped file identities are in the manifest.
